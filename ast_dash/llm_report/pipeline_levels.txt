================================================================================
HOME DYNAMICS PIPELINE — WHAT EACH LEVEL DOES
A mock evening pushed through derive_home_dynamics.py, Levels 0 to 4
================================================================================

    RAW  ->  L0 canonical observations
         ->  L1 semantic events        (audio labels, diarisation, emotion)
         ->  L2 activity episodes      (grouped by activity and room)
         ->  L3 person / social episodes
         ->  L4 daily and longitudinal dynamics

Every figure in this document is the actual output of running the eleven mock
rows below through ast_dash/llm_report/derive_home_dynamics.py unmodified.
Re-run it yourself with:

    python ast_dash/llm_report/pipeline_walkthrough.py

Counts at each stage:

    11 raw rows
    -> 11 L0 observations
    -> 40 L1 semantic events
    -> 12 L2 activity episodes
    -> 15 L3 person / social episodes
    ->  2 L4 day records

The structural fact that organises everything below: Levels 0 to 2 are keyed on
SOUND. Levels 3 and 4 are keyed on A PERSON. Everything the application claims
to do — monitoring family relationship dynamics — lives on the far side of that
boundary, and the join key across it is the speaker name.

================================================================================
LEVEL 0 — CANONICAL OBSERVATIONS          (11 rows in, 11 observations out)
================================================================================

THE JOB
Normalise the dashboard export. Nothing is interpreted here. Every field is
either copied, parsed, or recorded as missing.

WHAT IT DOES, PRECISELY

  1. Timestamps are parsed from any of three accepted formats and emitted as
     ISO. Rows whose timestamp will not parse are kept, not dropped, and sorted
     last.

  2. Labels are read from slots 1-3 with their probabilities. A nullish value
     -- "", none, null, nan, "no speech detected", unknown -- is moved into
     `raw_labels_discarded` rather than deleted, so the discard is auditable.

  3. Room is resolved in a fixed order: the event's own room field, then
     `device_room_map`, then unknown. Which one won is recorded in
     `room_source`, so a room inferred from a device is never mistaken for a
     room that was actually reported.

  4. Diarisation is parsed out of the transcription string, because the
     pipeline writes speaker identity into the text rather than into a column.
     Markers are matched anywhere in the string, and each turn's words run from
     the end of its own marker to the start of the next one.

  5. Two speech flags are kept deliberately separate:
       has_speech  -- a transcription exists at all
       has_words   -- the recogniser actually produced words
     A clip can be the first without being the second, and the distinction
     matters at L1 where it sets the confidence.

  6. Emotion is parsed from the string form "HAP (0.71)" into a label and a
     confidence. The literal "None" means no emotion was produced.

  7. Observations are sorted chronologically.

WHAT THE EXAMPLE PRODUCED

  Event 102 -- two speakers in one recording:

    room='Kitchen' (source: event)
    labels kept   : [('Speech', 0.71), ('Conversation', 0.52),
                     ('Inside, small room', 0.33)]
    has_speech=True  has_words=True  speakers=['Ilyas', 'Voula']
      turn 0.0-4.2s  'Ilyas': 'Θα φάμε στις οκτώ.'
      turn 4.5-9.1s  'Voula': 'Ναι, τελειώνω εδώ.'
    emotion       : {'label': 'happy', 'confidence': 0.71}

  Event 101 -- the discard is recorded, not silent:

    labels kept     : [('Frying (food)', 0.82), ('Dishes, pots, and pans', 0.41)]
    labels discarded: ['None']

  Event 109 -- room recovered from the device map:

    room='Bathroom' (source: device_map)
    (the event itself carried no room at all)

THE CONSEQUENTIAL ONE

  Event 108's transcript says "Unknown:". But `unknown` is in the nullish set,
  so the turn is kept with speaker=None and the observation ends with:

    has_speech=True  has_words=True  speakers=[]
      turn 0.0-2.1s  None: '...'

  It is still speech. It now carries no person. Follow this row down through
  the remaining levels -- it is the shape of 42% of real speech clips in the
  measured evaluation.


================================================================================
LEVEL 1 — SEMANTIC EVENTS                 (11 observations -> 40 events)
================================================================================

THE JOB
Translate each observation into meaning, through three independent extractors.
Nothing above this level knows which modality produced what. This is where a
future camera or door sensor would join the pipeline: by adding a fourth
extractor and changing nothing else.

WHAT IT DOES, PRECISELY

  A. AUDIO LABEL EXTRACTOR
     Each AudioSet label maps to zero or more semantic types through
     SEMANTIC_MAPPINGS, which is deliberately data rather than logic.
       - Several labels hitting the same type are combined: the strongest wins,
         plus a small deterministic corroboration bonus, clamped at 0.95.
       - A label with no mapping becomes `unknown_activity` and carries its own
         uncertainty note -- surfaced, not silently dropped.
       - A label mapped to the EMPTY LIST is deliberately ignored. That is how
         "Silence" and "Inside, small room" produce nothing at all.
       - Anything below min_semantic_confidence (0.05) is discarded.

  B. DIARISATION EXTRACTOR
     Runs independently of the labels, because the AudioSet head often scores
     Speech low on recordings that plainly contain words. Emits:
       - `speech_activity` at 0.90 when there are words, 0.70 when there are not
       - one `identified_speaker` per named speaker
       - `conversation_candidate` when two or more named speakers share one
         recording -- the strongest single piece of turn-taking evidence
         available

  C. EMOTION EXTRACTOR
     Emits `emotion_evidence` carrying the model's own confidence. Evidence,
     never a conclusion.

WHAT THE EXAMPLE PRODUCED

  Event 102 -- one row becomes seven semantic events:

    conversation_candidate     conf=0.52   via audio_labels
    speech_activity            conf=0.76   via audio_labels   <- Speech 0.71
                                                                 + corroboration
    speech_activity            conf=0.90   via diarization
    identified_speaker         conf=0.80   via diarization    person=Ilyas
    identified_speaker         conf=0.80   via diarization    person=Voula
    conversation_candidate     conf=0.75   via diarization
    emotion_evidence           conf=0.71   via emotion_model

    "Inside, small room" mapped to [] and produced nothing. Ambience, by design.

  Event 108 -- the unmapped label is surfaced, the speaker is not:

    speech_activity   conf=0.30   via audio_labels
    unknown_activity  conf=0.44   via audio_labels   (unmapped label: Zither)
    speech_activity   conf=0.90   via diarization
    ... and NO identified_speaker, because L0 found no name.

  Event 109 -- one row, four semantic types:

    bathroom_use_candidate    0.50
    faucet_use                0.70
    human_presence_candidate  0.21
    water_use                 0.70


================================================================================
LEVEL 2 — ACTIVITY EPISODES               (40 events -> 12 episodes)
================================================================================

THE JOB
Merge semantic events that are close in time, room and meaning, so that
"breathing, then water, then a flush" becomes one thing that happened rather
than three separate readings.

WHAT IT DOES, PRECISELY

  1. Group by (room, semantic type), and split whenever the gap between
     consecutive events exceeds that type's own allowance:
       speech_activity           120 s
       water_use                 300 s
       object_access             180 s
       cooking / food / media    600 s
       default                   300 s

  2. Supporting types never define an episode. `identified_speaker`,
     `emotion_evidence`, `human_presence_candidate`, `movement_candidate`,
     `faucet_use`, `object_handling_candidate` and `unknown_activity` describe
     HOW something was observed rather than WHAT happened, so they fold into
     whichever episode they overlap but never start one of their own.

  3. Composite rules relabel and nudge. When the semantic types present satisfy
     a rule -- and, for some rules, the room matches -- the episode is renamed
     and its confidence boosted. Rules are evaluated in order and the first
     match wins.

  4. Duplicate episodes are merged.

WHAT THE EXAMPLE PRODUCED

  Events 101 and 104 merge across six minutes, then get relabelled:

    act_20260830_0001  food_preparation_candidate  room=Kitchen
      19:00:00 -> 19:06:00   (360 s)  conf=0.92    <- cooking 0.82 + boost 0.10
      semantic types : ['cooking_candidate', 'emotion_evidence',
                        'food_activity_candidate', 'identified_speaker']
      from events    : [101, 104, 102, 103]

    The rule requires cooking_candidate or food_activity_candidate, supported by
    object_access -- which event 104's cupboard supplied. Note that the speaker
    and emotion evidence from events 102 and 103 folded in as support without
    changing what the episode IS.

  Event 109 satisfies two rules and yields two episodes:

    bathroom_use_candidate   conf=0.65    flush + water + presence
    personal_care_candidate  conf=0.925   water + faucet, room in {Bathroom, WC}

  The living room media episode hits the ceiling:

    media_use  conf=0.95    Television 0.88 + Music 0.40, then the media_use
                            composite -- clamped at max_semantic_confidence

WHERE THE ROOMS COME FROM

  The two bathroom episodes exist only because L0 resolved event 109's missing
  room through the device map. Had that lookup failed, both would have been
  room=None -- and co-presence at L3 explicitly refuses to treat a roomless
  episode as evidence.


================================================================================
LEVEL 3 — PERSON AND SOCIAL EPISODES      (-> 15 episodes)
================================================================================

THE JOB
The boundary of the whole system. Below here everything is keyed on sound; from
here up, everything is keyed on a person. Six episode types are produced, and
all of them join on the speaker name.

WHAT IT DOES, PRECISELY

  1. person_presence_evidence
     One per observation per named speaker. Confidence 0.80 for a configured
     household member, 0.50 for anyone else. Always carries the note "evidence
     of presence at this moment only; continuous presence not implied", and a
     non-member additionally carries "speaker not in configured household".

  2. speech_episode
     Turns are clustered while consecutive turns are within
     conversation_gap_seconds (120 s) AND in the same room. Records turn count,
     per-speaker turn counts, speaker changes measured on the ordered sequence,
     up to ten transcript excerpts, and an aggregated emotion summary.

  3. reciprocal_conversation_candidate
     Fires only when TWO OR MORE KNOWN speakers appear in one cluster. Adds turn
     balance (quieter party's turns over the louder party's), the response gaps
     between consecutive turns by different speakers, and a confidence that
     rises 0.05 per speaker change up to a 0.90 ceiling.

  4. single_person_speech_episode
     Exactly one known speaker in the cluster. Tagged "does not imply the person
     was alone".

  5. same_room_copresence_candidate
     Two different members with presence evidence in the same room within
     copresence_gap_seconds (300 s). Pairs are collected into windows and merged
     first, so a run of alternating speech yields one co-presence episode rather
     than one per pair of records. A roomless episode is never used.

  6. shared_media_candidate
     An L2 media episode with two or more people identified nearby. With exactly
     one person it becomes `media_use_with_unidentified_viewers` (0.50); with
     none it stays `media_use` (0.50) with "audience unknown".

WHAT THE EXAMPLE PRODUCED

  The kitchen exchange -- events 102 and 103 become a conversation:

    soc_20260830_0007  reciprocal_conversation_candidate  Kitchen  conf=0.65
      19:02:30 -> 19:04:03.8   participants=['Ilyas', 'Voula']
        estimated_turns      : {"Ilyas": 1, "Voula": 2}
        turn_balance         : 0.5      <- quieter party spoke half as often
        speaker_changes      : 1
        response_gaps_seconds: [0.3]
        emotion_summary      : {"dominant": "happy",
                                "distribution": {"happy": 0.721,
                                                 "neutral": 0.279}}
      ! turn counts derive from diarisation segments, not from continuous
        voice-activity timing

  The living room -- co-presence, then shared media:

    same_room_copresence_candidate   Rafael + Voula, span 90 s
    shared_media_candidate           participants=['Rafael', 'Voula']
                                     the TV episode, now with an audience

  Day two -- Ilyas alone:

    single_person_speech_episode  participants=['Ilyas']  turn_count: 1
      ! does not imply the person was alone

EVENT 108, CONCLUDED

  Its turn DID join the living-room speech cluster. turn_count is 3, and its
  '...' appears in the transcript excerpts. But it contributed:

      no person_presence_evidence
      no participant in any episode
      nothing to any pair

  because it had no name to join on. It inflates the appearance of activity
  while adding nothing that a person-level conclusion can be built from.

  That is the exact mechanism by which the measured 42% Unknown rate becomes
  missing relationships rather than visible gaps. And because
  reciprocal_conversation_candidate requires TWO known speakers, a real exchange
  with one end unattributed does not degrade gracefully -- it vanishes.


================================================================================
LEVEL 4 — DAILY AND LONGITUDINAL DYNAMICS  (-> 2 day records)
================================================================================

THE JOB
Aggregate one day at a time, per person and per pair, and state the limits of
the evidence alongside it. This is the object the LLM actually reads.

WHAT IT DOES, PRECISELY

  1. household_activity     episode counts by activity and by room, with the
                            standing note that episode durations span detected
                            evidence only

  2. individual_patterns    per member: speech episode count, presence evidence
                            count, rooms recognised in, an emotion distribution,
                            and a breakdown of social participation by episode
                            type. Only people the day actually evidences appear.

  3. pair_dynamics          per pair: conversations, co-presence, shared
                            activities, total interaction events, a mean turn
                            balance, a joint emotion summary, and an
                            evidence_strength of "moderate" at three or more
                            shared episodes, otherwise "limited"

  4. group_dynamics         multi-person episode count, largest group observed

  5. no_observation_intervals
                            every gap over no_observation_gap_seconds (1800 s),
                            carried up with the note that this is absence of
                            evidence, not evidence of silence

  6. data_quality           how many events had no room, how much speech went
                            unattributed, whether continuous monitoring is
                            known, and an overall evidence quality rating

  7. uncertainties          shipped as part of the payload, not as a footnote

  8. baseline_comparison    against prior days; refuses to compare below
                            min_baseline_days (7) of history

WHAT THE EXAMPLE PRODUCED (30 August)

    copresence_candidate_count : 1

WHAT THE DESIGN GETS RIGHT

  Event 108 is not swept away. It appears at L4 as
  unidentified_speech_event_count: 1, and the four observation gaps are reported
  as gaps rather than as a quiet house. The pipeline tells the model what it
  could not see, which is precisely what stops missing evidence being read as
  evidence of absence.


================================================================================
READING THIS AGAINST THE COMPONENT EVALUATION
================================================================================

Levels 0 through 2 are keyed on sound, and the components feeding them measured
well: sound labelling is correct at rank one on 82.4% of clips and within the
top three on 90.5%, and the speech gate drops no speech at all (100% recall).

Levels 3 and 4 are keyed on a person, and the component feeding them -- speaker
identity -- measured 51.1% accuracy, with 42% of speech clips left unnamed.

Event 108 is that gap in miniature. The pipeline handles it correctly at every
single step and still cannot produce a person from it, because there is no name
to join on. Everything the daily report says about individuals and pairs rests
on a join key that is currently right about half the time, and the failure is
silent: a conversation with one end unattributed does not arrive marked
uncertain, it does not arrive at all.

The one configuration decision that changes this is the speaker abstain. With it
in place, unattributed speech becomes unidentified_speech_event_count --
visible, countable, and correctly excluded from pair dynamics. Without it
(speaker_id.min_similarity = 0.0), every clip receives a name, and those same
clips arrive at L4 as named participants in conversations that may never have
happened.

See tests/diagnostics/run_0829_0830/figure_commentary.txt for the full
evaluation these figures come from.
